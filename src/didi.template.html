The brand, applied

Everything here is sampled from their Instagram: the red, the gold, the poster type, the “best for less” voice. The only thing I changed is what had to change to make it operable.

One flow, four taps: count what is left, build the order, send it. Counting and requesting are the same screen because splitting them is the step chefs skip.

  • Their yellow-on-red is 2.46:1. It works on Instagram because it is always poster-scale — at 13px it is unreadable. Dropping the ground one step to #A82713 takes it to 4.41, so the pairing survives at every size.
  • Yellow became the action colour. Ink on yellow is 11.66:1 — the strongest pair they own. So every primary button is yellow, and nothing else is.

What carries the brand

  • Poster headers. Gabarito Black, uppercase, −4% tracking, 88% leading — the stacked display type from their grid, with the repeated TASTY · YUMMY texture behind it.
  • The dishes are the products, shot by them. Jollof, fried rice, waakye, grilled chicken — cropped straight out of the @didijollof grid, not “chicken, tomatoes, spring onions”.
  • A live countdown. The 8:00 PM supplier cutoff is the reason this screen exists, so it ticks.
  • Stock as a bar against par, not a number to decode — 8 of 40 reads instantly as nearly empty. The notch on the bar is the minimum; fall left of it and the tag turns CRITICAL.
  • The chef types what is left. That count is the only editable number on the stock screen — no separate stock-check step, no extra tab. Tap it and the tag, the bar and the suggested order quantity all follow, so the request is derived from the count rather than guessed alongside it.
  • The count travels with the request. Every order line reads 8 left of 40 and every ops line 32 portions requested · 8 left — operations approves against what is actually in the kitchen, not a bare number.

Motion

  • Two springs, from Apple’s damping/response model: ζ 1.0 · 0.4s for navigation, ζ 0.8 · 0.3s for the order sheet. Same values are wired into the Figma prototype.
  • Feedback on pointer-down, not click. Bars fill with a stagger on arrival; quantities roll in the direction they moved.
  • All of it yields to prefers-reduced-motion.
